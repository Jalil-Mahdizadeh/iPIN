**Proposal to improve native PLM-interact — critical review and Arrhenius feasibility**

Prepared 28 September 2026. Scope: general cellular protein–protein interaction (PPI) prediction, principally human and established model-organism benchmarks. This proposal retains ESM-2-650M as the starting backbone and prioritizes three changes: better supervised data, an exactly symmetric pair predictor, and training that preserves long sequences.

**Recommendation.** Keep the central contribution of PLM-interact: jointly encode the two proteins and update the complete encoder. Invest first in the reliability of the training signal and evaluation, then in pair symmetry and sequence coverage. These changes address observed weaknesses; replacing the backbone with a larger model or adding predicted structures has a less certain return. Full end-to-end PPI fine-tuning is technically feasible on Arrhenius, preferably starting with one four-GPU node. A credible accuracy claim still requires new controlled experiments.

Here, “full retraining” means restarting PPI training from the original pretrained ESM-2 weights and updating all useful backbone parameters and the task head. It does **not** mean pretraining a protein language model from scratch. Starting from the released PPI checkpoint would retain its supervised exposure and compromise newly constructed holdouts. No retraining was performed for this proposal. The only new GPU work was synthetic forward/backward resource profiling, with no optimizer, zero parameter updates, and an explicit check that all saved state tensors remained unchanged.

**Evidence reviewed.** I reread the [published paper](literature/plm-interact/article.pdf), [supplementary information](literature/plm-interact/supplementary-information.pdf), [reporting summary](literature/plm-interact/reporting-summary.pdf), and [peer-review reports and author responses](literature/plm-interact/peer-review.pdf). I cross-checked the relevant source-data findings against our [checkpoint reproduction report](plm-interact-reproducability/REPORT.md), its numerical audits, and the [publication-archived training script](plm-interact-reproducability/provenance/publication-code/PLMinteract/train_mlm.py). The paper is Liu et al., *Nature Communications* 16, 9012 (2025), [DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-64512-w](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-64512-w). New resource measurements and their scripts are retained in [improvement-review](literature/plm-interact/improvement-review/).

**What the evidence supports—and what needs improvement.**

The architecture is unusually economical conceptually: concatenate `CLS A EOS B EOS`, run a shared ESM-2 cross-encoder, and classify the final CLS representation using ReLU and a linear layer. It fine-tunes the encoder with a classification objective and an auxiliary masked-language-modeling (MLM) objective. The paper's Methods and code specify MLM:classification weights of 1:10; the Results prose reverses that description. The released checkpoints substantially reproduce the principal benchmark metrics. This supports the model as a research starting point, while leaving training reproducibility and generalization as separate questions.

These comparisons use the authors' identity annotations, not newly computed alignments.

Two further qualifications matter. First, the masking evidence supports testing MLM as a regularizer, not assuming it always helps: deposited mouse AP is 0.907840 without masking and 0.903963 at 15% masking. The exact selected thresholds and their selection data are not sufficiently documented for our audit to reconstruct that comparison. McNemar also tests paired classification outcomes, not improvement in AP. Second, the illustrated structures are selected successes, and several have low interface confidence. They do not establish interface accuracy or justify treating predicted complexes as reliable new training labels. **Priority 1: rebuild the supervised corpus around evidence quality and genuinely held-out proteins.**

This has the highest expected scientific value. A sequence-only model can exploit protein identity, study frequency, family similarity and sampling artifacts without learning the intended interaction relationship. Bernett et al. experimentally demonstrated these problems for earlier PPI predictors, motivating stronger controls; their findings should not be read as a direct demonstration that PLM-interact learns only shortcuts. [Bernett et al., 2024](https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/25/2/bbae076/7621029).

Define the biological target before merging databases. For a binary physical-interaction predictor, distinguish direct binary evidence, membership in a larger complex, and functional association. STRING's physical evidence is useful, but a database-level physical label should not automatically be interpreted as a measured direct interface. Use versioned human/model-organism records from the original sources and evidence-rich resources such as IntAct/IMEx; retain assay, publication, sequence version, isoform and complex-expansion provenance. EMBL-EBI's interaction schema explicitly supports interaction type, detection method, negative-interaction flags and complex expansion, making these distinctions operationally possible. [EMBL-EBI interaction fields and evidence aggregation](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/Tools/webservices/psicquic/view/help.xhtml).

The proposed data change has four concrete parts:

1. **Resolve redundancy and contradictory evidence.** Map identifiers to versioned sequences and group unordered sequence pairs, preserving their underlying evidence records. Prevent the same experiment from being counted repeatedly through several databases. Keep conflicting outcomes with their assay/context information; exclude unresolved contradictions from the primary binary objective or place them in an explicitly ambiguous stratum. Do not collapse biologically distinct isoforms merely because their gene identifiers match. Include supported homomeric interactions as a reported stratum rather than silently excluding them from the general PPI task.
2. **Split before generating training pairs or augmented orders.** Construct protein homology groups and allocate groups to train/validation/test. A strict test pair should have both endpoints absent from training and no qualifying training homolog under the prespecified identity/coverage rule. Use a documented 40%-identity protocol as an initial comparison with the paper, audit residual cross-split similarities and shared domains, and report how much data partitioning discards. Clustering alone does not prove absence of all remote homology. Add a separately reported publication-time holdout using first supporting evidence dates, not merely database update dates. Preserve whole experimental groups where practical; quantify remaining dependence when protein and publication grouping cannot both be satisfied.
3. **Treat negative evidence honestly.** Use experimentally supported noninteractions where applicable to the intended assay/context, while recognizing their limited coverage. Retain randomly sampled unobserved pairs as *unlabeled-derived training negatives*. Compare uniform sampling with a mixture that reduces endpoint-frequency and length shortcuts. Keep the evaluation population and its sampling policy fixed across models. A degree-matched training sampler is a robustness intervention, not a claim about the natural distribution of all candidate pairs. Do not automatically label the model's highest-scoring unknown pairs as negatives: this can preferentially punish undiscovered positives.
4. **Make the objective reflect confidence and the intended operating point.** Start with ordinary BCE and transparent evidence/sampling weights. Validate the negative weight, class weight and calibration separately; do not combine oversampling and large positive weighting without accounting for their combined effect. A positive-unlabeled loss is a secondary sensitivity experiment if its class-prior and missing-label assumptions can be defended. Sparse, biased interaction observations do not automatically satisfy those assumptions. Fit score calibration and decision thresholds only on validation data representative of the stated use case.

Use a consistent target definition across training and evaluation. Tightening training to direct binary evidence while leaving a co-complex test unchanged would confound an improvement claim; report those evidence strata separately. The authors already supply a larger STRING V12 model and use experimental evidence and redundancy filtering, so simply adding more STRING rows is not the proposed contribution. The added value is provenance, split construction, negative-label treatment and control of sampling artifacts.

The locked primary holdout should remain outside every supervised stage, including any auxiliary PPI or human mutation fine-tuning. For mutation evaluation, group related variants and canonical interaction families; the existing mutation audit finds **no exact training/test triplet or canonical-pair overlap**, but finds both individual proteins seen during training in 271/841 test cases and at least one in 643/841. That supports testing stronger generalization, not alleging exact-pair leakage. ESM-2's unsupervised pretraining exposure must also be disclosed; a PPI-label-disjoint split does not make its sequences unseen during language-model pretraining. [Mutation split audit](plm-interact-reproducability/results/mutation-split-audit.json).

**Hypothesis and risk.** Better evidence and less exploitable sampling should improve performance on unseen protein families and calibration under realistic candidate selection. It can reduce the apparent AP on an easier, biased benchmark, and strict curation can leave too few diverse positives. Measure retained pairs, families, assays, degree distribution and label prevalence before committing to the full run. Accept the data change on independently held-out predictive value, not on dataset size alone.

**Priority 2: make the cross-encoder an exactly symmetric predictor of an unordered PPI.**

For the task “do A and B physically interact?”, the two input orders represent the same target. Training on both orders, as the authors already do, encourages this property but does not guarantee it. The observed order flips make this a concrete model defect to address.

The lowest-risk architecture change keeps one shared ESM-2 encoder and one shared classifier. For its two orientation logits, define:

```text
z_AB = f_theta(CLS A EOS B EOS)
z_BA = f_theta(CLS B EOS A EOS)
z_pair = (z_AB + z_BA) / 2
p_pair = sigmoid(z_pair)
classification_loss = BCEWithLogits(z_pair, y)
```

Train the classification objective on this pooled score and average the two auxiliary MLM losses if MLM is retained. Fix this logit-pooling convention in advance: averaging probabilities is a different predictor. In deterministic evaluation, with the same sequence handling, swapping A and B merely swaps the two terms and leaves the result invariant up to numerical tolerance. Apply any fitted calibration to the pooled score. Couple augmentation policies across orientations; padding, truncation or preprocessing must not introduce a new order dependence.

This is a constrained, shared-weight two-orientation cross-encoder, not a larger backbone. Its guaranteed benefit is removal of arbitrary input-order disagreement; a gain in AP is a hypothesis. Training with an order-consistency penalty alone would only encourage symmetry. Likewise, a symmetric pooling head on top of order-dependent contextual embeddings does not by itself make the complete model invariant.

Evaluate three versions: the ordinary trained reference, that same reference with pooled inference, and a model retrained with the pooled objective. The second requires no retraining and establishes how much of any gain comes merely from combining predictions. A deterministic canonical sequence-order baseline is another inexpensive check. If pooled inference is as good as pooled-objective training, report that result and avoid crediting retraining for the gain.

Two orders approximately double inference encoder work relative to the native one-order prediction. The paper already doubles training examples by presenting both orders, so training cost should be counted in actual orientation passes rather than automatically multiplied by two again. Report both distinct pairs per optimizer update and orientation passes per update. For example, 64 distinct pairs evaluated in both orders constitute 128 orientation passes, not 128 independent pairs. Holding token exposures fixed and holding distinct-pair batch size fixed are different comparisons.

**Success criterion.** Near-zero per-pair order gap and zero meaningful order-induced decision flips are required. AP, calibration and precision at the chosen operating point must remain competitive on the locked holdout. If cost is unacceptable, investigate a single-pass encoder with chain-exchange-equivariant positions/attention and pooling later; that is a substantially larger architectural change requiring its own validation.

**Priority 3: train on long proteins without silently deleting interaction information.**

Length handling is an empirical weakness of the native workflow. Our Bernett reproduction found that an undocumented 3,570-token inference cap explains the deposited scores extremely closely, while full sequences still reproduce the displayed metrics. This was a post-hoc implementation diagnostic, not evidence that 3,570 is a biologically optimal context length. The mutation audit demonstrates that shorter truncation can remove the entire sequence difference being evaluated. [Bernett diagnostic](plm-interact-reproducability/results/bernett-cap-diagnostic.json), [mutation truncation audit](plm-interact-reproducability/results/mutation-truncation-audit.json).

Use length buckets and adaptive microbatches, retaining complete sequences for the supported range. With eager dense attention, batch planning must account for padded length squared, not just the sum of token counts. Use BF16 autocast and activation checkpointing where required, accumulating gradients to a documented effective batch. Keep long examples in the training distribution and sample/report length strata explicitly. Removing a length cap without adding representative long training examples does not establish long-context generalization, even though ESM-2's rotary positions permit execution beyond its original context setting.

Share the backbone computation between the MLM and classification heads. The archived training code currently computes an MLM encoder pass and then a second encoder pass on the **same masked input** for the classifier. With its zero attention/hidden dropout configuration, a shared pass represents the same mathematical objective, subject to floating-point differences. Our small FP32 qualification found identical scalar loss and relative gradient L2 difference of 1.74e-6 across 538 gradient tensors; the maximum absolute element difference was 0.00128 on this high-gradient synthetic fixture. The optimization therefore merits broader qualification, rather than a claim of bitwise training equivalence. It is an efficiency change common to all experimental arms, not evidence of improved accuracy. [Profile and qualification](literature/plm-interact/improvement-review/synthetic-profile.json).

Efficient attention is a further option, but the supplied Transformers 4.40.1 ESM implementation reports `_supports_sdpa=False`. Installing a recent PyTorch or setting an attention flag does not by itself convert this encoder. A separately versioned implementation would need to preserve the ESM rotary/scaling behavior, padding masks, bidirectional attention and token-dropout behavior, and pass forward/gradient comparisons. PyTorch documents both the available attention backends and numerical differences between them. Checkpointing already provides a working memory-saving path in the current image. [PyTorch 2.8 SDPA](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.8/generated/torch.nn.functional.scaled_dot_product_attention.html), [activation checkpointing](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.8/checkpoint.html).

For sequences beyond a qualified limit, report coverage and handle them explicitly. Domain/window aggregation may become necessary, but it changes the model's biological assumptions and can miss interactions requiring separated regions. It should be a later comparison, not an unreported fallback. Any human variant evaluation must verify that the relevant difference survives preprocessing and report the full eligible test population alongside legacy subsets.

**Hypothesis and risk.** Restoring the long tail should improve coverage and performance for long or multidomain proteins. It may have little effect on overall AP when those cases are rare, and it increases compute cost. Separate a same-data comparison of length handling from a comparison that adds previously excluded training pairs; otherwise their effects cannot be distinguished. Systems improvements alone should preserve predictions, while the length/data change needs an accuracy experiment.

**The trainer needs correction before any scientific retraining.**

These observations concern the publication-archived script, not unavailable logs of every published training run. They should be treated as prerequisites to a controlled reference implementation, rather than evidence that all released checkpoints were trained incorrectly.

| Archived behavior | Required action |
| --- | --- |
| `train()` has an inner batch size default of 1; `main()` does not pass through the CLI batch size to it. The inner token-only DataLoader shuffles rows, and `forward()` retains only its last batch. | Remove the nested loader and mask tokenized examples in a collator that carries labels and sample IDs together. Increasing the outer microbatch unchanged can produce one output against multiple labels; merely increasing the inner batch can still misalign shuffled features with unchanged labels. The README's microbatch-1 example avoids this particular batching problem. |
| Accumulation steps on `batch_idx % accumulation == 0`, starting at index 0. No end-of-epoch flush is visible. | Step after the intended number of microbatches; handle the final partial accumulation with correct normalization. Qualify gradients against a true larger batch and check that DDP synchronizes on the intended update. |
| Scheduler total steps are computed from microbatches, although the scheduler advances on optimizer updates. AMP skip detection compares the scale after it has already been updated. | Define the schedule in actual optimizer updates and handle skipped updates correctly. Store the exact schedule and resume position. |
| Masking is hard-coded to 22%, while the paper selects 15%. BCE uses `pos_weight=10`, separately from the 10-fold classification-loss coefficient. | Expose and log these as distinct settings. Include an unmasked/no-MLM control selected through validation. Audit class weighting separately for the balanced Bernett regime; do not assume 10 remains appropriate there. |
| The exposed seed initializes Python randomness; complete PyTorch/NumPy/rank RNG handling is not evident. Saved epoch checkpoints omit full RNG/sampler state. | Seed every relevant generator, record rank behavior and save model, optimizer, scheduler, RNG, sampler and update counters for resumable runs. Test interrupted versus uninterrupted continuation. |

See the [archived trainer](plm-interact-reproducability/provenance/publication-code/PLMinteract/train_mlm.py), [DDP helper](plm-interact-reproducability/provenance/publication-code/PLMinteract/utils/ddp.py), and [README training example](plm-interact-reproducability/provenance/publication-code/README.md). With positive-weighted BCE, a score threshold of 0.5 is not automatically a calibrated neutral probability threshold for the target population. Calibration must follow the actual sampling and weighting scheme.

The human mutation task also needs one explicit scoring contract. The released implementation uses a sigmoid of a mutant-minus-wild-type **logit difference**; the paper writes a log ratio of their sigmoid probabilities. These can rank cases differently. Our previous reproduction quantified that difference. Neither score is a measured binding-affinity change. Keep this as a separate downstream validation task rather than claiming that better binary PPI AP automatically improves mutation-effect prediction. [Scoring comparison](plm-interact-reproducability/REPORT.md).

**Experiments that would make an improvement claim credible.**

First build the corrected native reference from the same original ESM-2 initialization. Apply the correctness and shared-computation fixes to every arm. Use one locked partition and the same evaluation target throughout; data variants may change only the allowed training portion. Limit all hyperparameter selection, calibration and stopping to validation. Use the old cross-species/Bernett results as secondary historical comparisons, with supervised training overlap and protocol differences disclosed. An expanded model trained on test-species labels cannot claim the original human-only cross-species transfer setting.

The following is a focused campaign for **one** defined data regime. Single-seed ablations screen components; they do not establish seed-robust component effects. Advance only promising combinations and repeat an individual ablation if its independent contribution becomes a headline claim.

| Arm | Change relative to corrected native reference | Initial full runs |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| R | Native architecture, original-style supervised sampling and documented length policy | 3 seeds |
| D | Priority 1 training-data package | 1 seed |
| S | Priority 2 pooled training objective; also evaluate R with pooled inference | 1 seed |
| L | Priority 3 length coverage; separate same-data and added-data effects when interpreting it | 1 seed |
| D+S+L | Validated combined model | 3 seeds |
| Matched two-branch control | Same pretrained ESM-2, fully fine-tuned separately encoded proteins, symmetric pair head | 1 seed |
| No-MLM control | Reference with unmasked classification-only training | 1 seed |
| **Total** | Full-length/sampling sub-ablations or extra benchmark-specific training add runs | **11 runs** |

The end-to-end two-branch control is particularly important: comparisons with frozen-embedding methods change both the interaction architecture and the number of trainable parameters. It tests whether joint token context adds value beyond fully fine-tuning ESM-2. Compare both training exposures and compute, since separate encoding and joint encoding have different attention costs. Also include cheap endpoint-frequency and sequence-similarity controls using training information only.

Prespecify AP (the paper's “AUPR” implementation), AUROC, precision at a validation-chosen operating point, recall, and calibration diagnostics such as Brier score/reliability curves. Always state prevalence and candidate-sampling policy; AP is not directly comparable between 1:1 and 1:10 benchmarks. Report novel-protein/family, length, assay and species strata with counts, plus paired order gaps and prediction coverage. Preserve raw logits to avoid unnecessary sigmoid saturation ties. Bootstrap held-out protein/family or experimental groups as appropriate to the dependence structure, not duplicated rows as independent samples, and report variability across seeds separately.

A reasonable provisional practical target is at least **+0.01 absolute AP** on the new strict primary holdout, with a paired 95% uncertainty interval favoring the combined model and consistent gains across the three seeds. Set that target and any acceptable secondary-performance loss before opening the test labels; revise it beforehand if dataset size makes it uninformative. Symmetry and coverage have separate success criteria even if AP does not improve. Avoid claiming that all three components help unless the ablations support it.

I would defer larger backbones, complex-structure supervision, network-derived input features and extra multitask heads. They add compute, evidence-quality or leakage questions before the current weaknesses have been resolved. Predicted structures can later be evaluated as confidence-aware features against a strong sequence reference; selected structural illustrations are insufficient motivation for assuming a gain.

Multi-node DDP remains unqualified. NAISS documentation calls out Slingshot integration for multi-node containers and provides GPU programming environments with NCCL/OFI components. An in-container library version by itself does not prove the fast network path is working. **Runtime and allocation budget: planning scenarios, not measured training completion times.**

The paper reports 31.1 hours on 16 A100-80GB GPUs for ten humanV11 epochs; Supplementary Table 1 gives 496.9 GPU-hours for the selected 650M objective. The reported STRING V12 run takes 86.4 hours on 16 A100s. The Bernett reviewer response specifies five epochs, illustrating why the exact training regime must be recorded separately from the general ten-epoch description. These are useful external scale references, not transferable GH200 runtime measurements. [Paper Methods](literature/plm-interact/article.pdf), [Supplementary Table 1](literature/plm-interact/supplementary-information.pdf), [Bernett response](literature/plm-interact/peer-review.pdf).

For a transparent humanV11-sized planning unit, use 421,792 distinct input rows, ten epochs and two orientations: **8,435,840 orientation exposures**. Let `q` be sustained orientation examples per GPU-second before distributed scaling losses and `eta` the distributed efficiency:

```text
wall_hours = (N_pairs * epochs * orientations) / (GPUs * q * eta * 3600)
GPU_hours  = GPUs * wall_hours
```

Assume `q = 8–16` for a workload dominated by short, efficiently bucketed pairs, informed by—but lower than much of—the synthetic batch-4 throughput. Assume `q = 2–8` for a more expensive mixed-length workload. The second range is not a bound: a workload dominated by 7,426-token inputs could be slower. Assume efficiency 0.70–0.90 on four GPUs and 0.50–0.80 on eight; neither range has been measured for this trainer. Include loading/optimizer overhead when replacing `q` with pilot measurements.

The wider eight-GPU GPU-hour range reflects assumed communication losses; more GPUs do not automatically reduce allocation cost. These are orientation-count budgets: do not double them again solely because the symmetric model uses two orientations. Scale them linearly with actual row count and epoch count, then revise for the measured length mixture. The original shorter Bernett regime has fewer exposures, but its longer inputs prevent estimating its cost by dataset-size scaling alone. Larger curated corpora have not yet been constructed or counted. Unrounded calculations and assumptions are retained in [budget-scenarios.json](literature/plm-interact/improvement-review/budget-scenarios.json).

For the 11-run matrix on one four-GPU node, these assumptions imply about **1,800–4,600 GPU-hours** if every run resembled the short scenario, or **3,600–18,400 GPU-hours** if every run resembled the mixed/long scenario. Add an explicit allowance, for example 25%, for validation, checkpointing, engineering retries and failed experiments; queue time affects calendar time separately. A real campaign mixes these workloads. These ranges are a resource request basis, not confirmation that the current project can fund the work. The remaining allocation must be checked against authoritative project accounting before scheduling a campaign.

Many plausible runs exceed the 72-hour wall limit. Plan 24–48-hour restartable job segments, with periodic atomic checkpoints and scheduler-signal handling. A weights-plus-Adam checkpoint has about 7.83 GB of tensor state before auxiliary state and file overhead; retaining a handful of restart points and the selected model, rather than every epoch of every run, keeps storage manageable. Reserve roughly 100–250 GB for initial data artifacts, base weights, logs and retained checkpoints, and revise upward for concurrent runs or larger corpora. Keep bulk data/checkpoints in project storage; use node-local scratch for disposable caches where appropriate. [NAISS storage guidance](https://hpc.pages.naiss.se/user-documentation/support-docs/arrhenius_hpc/data_management/storage/).

**Recommended execution order after this proposal.** Prepare and freeze the evidence/split manifest; build and qualify the corrected reference trainer; run a short representative full-update pilot on one GPU and then one four-GPU node; measure optimizer-state memory, pooled-objective memory, real length-weighted throughput, resume fidelity and DDP synchronization. Recalculate the budget from those measurements. Then run single-seed component screens, commit three seeds to the reference and selected combined model, and open the locked test once decisions are fixed. A second node should follow demonstrated scaling value, not precede it.

The current evidence supports proceeding to that controlled retraining program. It establishes memory feasibility and identifies concrete, testable improvements. It does not yet establish an accuracy gain, an allocation balance, or a validated multi-node training runtime. The original SIF, released checkpoints and prior reproduction results remain intact.
